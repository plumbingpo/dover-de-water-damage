Where Water Damage Starts in Wilmington Properties

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Wilmington households. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Wilmington, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Most of these are avoided by getting the drain cleaned annually ahead of heavy use.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. It reaches ground-level rooms, garages and walkout lower levels on the downhill side before anything else. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.
